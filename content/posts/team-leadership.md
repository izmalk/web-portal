I've had two opportunities to build and lead teams. In both cases, I created cross-functional teams of highly skilled professionals from scratch, and I'm proud of what we achieved.

<!--more-->

After receiving my PhD in Technology, I became head of a research department in the institute's Information and Communication Centre. My task was to build a team to research and design automated systems.

After establishing the team and its test lab infrastructure, I was also appointed head of the institute's IT department. I continued to lead the research department, managing both system administrators and researchers. This gave me the chance to contribute to scientific projects and help people solve practical IT problems. It was an important period in my development as a manager and a specialist with a broad range of skills.

My second opportunity came when I was asked to form a new documentation team for VK Cloud Solutions, then known as Mail.Ru Cloud Solutions. I had already been with the company for more than a year. Building this team also gave me the chance to develop my leadership skills in an agile environment, having previously worked in a more traditional waterfall environment.

I built and managed a cross-functional team of 11 professionals: five technical writers, four analysts, one QA specialist and one DocOps specialist. I established processes for daily, weekly and monthly meetings; planning for individual sprints and the next one to three months; sprint retrospectives; style guide discussions; and regular feedback.

I also lead the company's technical writers' guild, a professional group that meets every two weeks to share experience and discuss our work.
